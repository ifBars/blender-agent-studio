# Complete scene composition and decoration

Use this for full environments, interiors and scene decoration. Scale the
workflow to the scene; a short list of zones and asset families is sufficient.
Preserve useful natural modeling choices rather than making every scene a
large paperwork or validation exercise.

## Solve the picture and the space together

Write a one-sentence scene intention: what should the viewer notice, what is
the place used for, and what mood makes that readable? Choose a coherent
reference direction for architecture, furniture, material palette and light.
Block out the complete usable space at real scale. Author the hero camera,
an opposite view and a view of the main secondary area before decorating.
Check focal hierarchy, foreground/midground/background separation, silhouette,
access, support heights and important negative spaces in inexpensive renders.

Judge the usable layout and camera together at delivery size. Circulation must
remain clear without pushing the main activity zone into a small distant
corner or leaving an oversized empty center. Correct layout, camera position
or lens when the focal zone reads weakly; adding more clutter is not a layout
repair. Compare the main furniture's silhouette and upholstery as closely as
architectural trim and prop count.

The hero camera guides the composition; secondary views reveal whether it
depends on hiding incomplete construction. Respect the brief's scope: an
interior render does not need an invented exterior, while an inspectable game
scene needs finished promised reverse surfaces.

## Build good pieces before multiplying them

Keep a compact coverage list: family, intended identity/finish, instances,
support, and remaining defect. Build architecture and supports first, then a
representative of each furniture/prop family. Review its silhouette, broad
surfaces, joints, material separation and back at the intended view distance.
A primitive with a bevel and a material is still a blockout when its shape
needs contour, thickness, joinery or a readable functional feature.

Choose construction methods from the form: shaped profiles and connected
surfaces for seating and garments, curves for straps/cables with attachment
points, coherent frames and panels for architecture. Decorative microdetail
does not replace the object's primary shape. Replicate after the family is
convincing; vary orientation, content and wear deliberately without damaging
its construction or style.

Inspect the representative's evaluated topology before multiplying it. Read
per-mesh invalid-element counts, not just whether Blender saved the file.
Revolved profiles need shared pole vertices; leaf tips and thin panels must
not collapse after modifiers. Size bevels against the affected feature's
thickness, including small pieces inside an accumulated mesh. Repair the
common construction helper before regenerating instances, then confirm the
intended contours, material boundaries and contacts still read in context.

## Decorate with relationships

Group objects around a credible use: reading, serving, packing, repairing or
displaying. Use varied heights and density, intentional gaps, and restrained
accent colors. Avoid uniform scatter, identical clutter on every surface,
and arbitrary props placed solely to increase object count. Keep clean areas
that support the composition. Detail budget should follow view distance and
functional importance while every promised family reaches finished quality.

Derive placements from support and attachment anchors, including object
extent, local orientation and fitted clearances. A center at table height
does not put the object's bottom on the table. Inspect contacts using low
side views and local context. Bounds identify candidates; use evaluated
geometry, surface samples or visible contact shadows to investigate uncertain
relationships. Support is not proved by a parent name or overlapping AABBs.

## Review finish in authored lighting

Review hero, reverse and secondary views after coherent batches. Keep the
scene's actual lights and world when assessing mood, glass, fabrics and
material response. Use standardized lighting only for separate geometry
questions. Fix weak shape, fit, texture scale and material response before
adding noise, grading, bloom or extra clutter. Scanned textures/HDRIs can
serve realistic briefs when allowed; keep their sources and dependencies
reproducible. Low-poly intent should remain deliberate and coherent.

Review broad floor reflections and texture repetition in every authored view.
A physically possible glossy window reflection can still dominate the scene;
match roughness, grain scale and lighting to the reference and focal hierarchy.
Judge scanned maps by their fitted appearance rather than their provenance.

At each review, name the most important visible defect, its likely source
parameter and the smallest useful correction. Check whether a correction
lost previously good form, materials or composition. Reserve time to finish
secondary families and integration before the final render/export. Inspect
close/context views of every family; expand to every instance when placement
or variation can introduce new failures. Do not approve a coverage row just
because it exists or passes a triangle limit.

## Deliver to the intended use

Render-only: durable source, self-contained .blend, authored beauty views and
render settings. Game scene: also fresh-import the requested export and review
layout, orientation, UVs and portable materials. Keep technical compliance,
art quality and engine-specific performance evidence separate. A beautiful
hero image does not excuse an unfinished reverse zone; numerical perfection
does not establish an appealing composition.
